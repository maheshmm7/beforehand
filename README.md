# Beforehand

**Know what a decision costs before you make it.**

A planning assistant for weddings, birthdays and community events. Every other planner is a
recorder: you decide, it stores. Beforehand is a simulator. Invite fifty more people and it tells
you, right then, that it is another ₹76,275, that two deadlines just moved closer, and that you now
need a second serving line — before you commit to any of it.

## The challenge, and how this answers it

**Vertical:** Challenge 5 — Celebration & Event Planner. Weddings, birthdays and community events.

_"Create a GenAI-powered planning assistant that helps users organize weddings, birthdays, and
community events with budgets, timelines, vendor checklists, and personalized ideas."_

The four named artefacts are not four screens bolted together. They all fall out of one engine:

| Required artefact      | How it is produced here                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Budgets**            | Derived from the event model, never typed in. Every line is `estimated`, `quoted` or `paid`, so you always know how much of the plan can still be changed for free.  |
| **Timelines**          | Two of them. Decision deadlines computed from how much notice each thing needs, and a run of show for the day itself, scheduled backwards from the one fixed moment. |
| **Vendor checklists**  | Generated by the model, so it cannot omit something your plan implies. Each entry carries the arithmetic behind its figure and the vendor's own quoted wording.      |
| **Personalized ideas** | Asked for against what the plan can still absorb, and filtered again on the server. An idea you cannot afford is never shown.                                        |

## The working principle

An event is a graph of coupled decisions, not a checklist. The engine holds:

- **variables** — the levers (guest count, venue class, menu tier, date);
- **quantities** — values derived from them (tables, households, guests travelling in);
- **cost rules, constraints, tasks and lead times** — declared as data over those.

Change a lever and the whole graph is re-evaluated, then compared with the version you last
accepted. The difference — which lines moved, which deadlines shifted, which tasks appeared, which
constraint broke — is the product. That difference is what the strip along the bottom of every plan
screen reports, before you accept it.

### Three states for every rupee

A line starts as an **estimate** from the model. Paste what a vendor actually sent and it becomes a
**quote**, with their own words kept beside it and the old estimate kept underneath. Once an advance
is paid it becomes **paid**, and the decision behind it is no longer free to change. The simulator
gets more accurate as the date gets closer, which is exactly when accuracy matters.

### Deadlines that actually move

Lead times are expressions over the plan, not constants. A longer guest list takes longer to print,
post and cater for, so the date it must be frozen by comes forward with it. This is why the blast
radius can report "2 deadlines shifted" and mean it.

### One engine, three kinds of event

The difference between the packs is data, and each has a different master variable:

| Event           | Master variable        | What ripples                                                                                             |
| --------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wedding         | Headcount              | Money. Nearly every cost is per-head, across several functions with different subsets.                   |
| Birthday        | The clock              | The schedule. A fixed slot the cake, the food and the entertainment have to fit inside.                  |
| Community event | Volunteers and permits | Capacity and compliance — plus sponsorship, carried as negative lines, so a plan can come out in credit. |

## Where the AI is, and where it is deliberately not

Gemini does two things: it reads what you paste, and it proposes ideas. **It never does the
arithmetic.** Every figure is computed by ordinary TypeScript, which is why each one can show its
working ("770 servings × 450"), why the numbers cannot be hallucinated, and why the whole
application still works with the assistant switched off.

What the model returns is validated against the same Zod schema the client was promised. A reply
that invents a field, returns the wrong type, or wanders outside the contract is refused rather
than shown. Headroom is enforced on the server after the model answers, not merely requested in the
prompt.

Pasted text is fenced inside a boundary that changes every request, labelled as data, and the model
is told to ignore instructions inside it. Phone numbers, email addresses and URLs are stripped in
the browser and again on all three routes before anything leaves the machine.

## The interface

A landing page that demonstrates the product rather than describing it: the hero carries a working
simulator running the real engine, so dragging the guest count on the marketing page recomputes the
same model the app uses.

Inside a plan, four screens:

- **Budget** — the four headline figures, the levers that move them, and where the money goes as a
  ring and a list. Nothing on it folds, grows or scrolls while you read it.
- **Timeline** — decision deadlines in the order they fall, and the run of show for the day.
- **Vendors** — every line nobody has promised yet, with the arithmetic behind each figure and a
  place to record what a vendor actually sent.
- **Ideas** — suggestions asked for against what the plan can still absorb.

Playfair Display for anything that announces itself and Inter for everything that has to be read
quickly, both self-hosted so the page loads nothing from another domain. Light and dark themes, each
defined once as tokens, with a control that remembers your choice. Sections settle in as they are
scrolled to, money counts from the old figure to the new one, and the slider is a gauge — all of it
skipped entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and none of it required for the page to work.

The ring's six colours are stepped so that every neighbouring pair clears both a normal-vision
separation floor and the three common kinds of colour blindness, against each theme's own surface.
The list beside it carries the name, the share and the figure, so identity never rests on hue.

## Running it locally

**Requirements:** Node 20 or newer, and npm.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/maheshmm7/beforehand.git
cd beforehand
npm install
cp .env.example .env    # add a Gemini API key to switch the assistant on
npm run dev             # the web app, on http://localhost:5180
npm run dev:api         # the API, in a second terminal, on http://localhost:8787
```

Vite proxies `/api` to the second process, so the browser only ever talks to one origin.

The app is fully usable with **no key**: the simulator, both timelines and the vendor ledger are all
deterministic. Only "Set it up for me", "Read it for me" and "Suggest ideas" need one.

## Deploying to Vercel

The repository is configured for Vercel as it stands. The static site is built by Vite into `dist`,
and `api/index.ts` mounts the same Express application as one serverless function behind `/api/*`.

1. **Import the repository** at [vercel.com/new](https://vercel.com/new). Vercel reads
   [`vercel.json`](vercel.json), so the build command (`npm run build`), the output directory
   (`dist`) and the function settings are already set. Leave the framework preset on its detected
   value.
2. **Add the environment variable** `GEMINI_API_KEY` under _Settings → Environment Variables_, for
   Production, Preview and Development. Nothing else is required; every other variable has a
   working default. See [Environment variables](#environment-variables).
3. **Deploy.** The first build takes roughly a minute.
4. **Check the deployment** once it is live:
   - `https://<your-app>.vercel.app/` — the landing page, with the hero simulator running.
   - `https://<your-app>.vercel.app/api/health` — should answer
     `{"status":"ok","assistant":"on"}`. If it says `"assistant":"off"`, the key is not set.
   - Create a plan and move a lever. That path needs no key at all.
   - Open the Ideas screen and ask for suggestions. That one proves the key is wired.

### What is already handled

| Concern                      | How                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Build and output             | `buildCommand` and `outputDirectory` pinned in `vercel.json`; `npm ci` from the committed lockfile                                                                   |
| API routing                  | One function at `api/index.ts`; `/api/(.*)` rewritten to it, so every route is served by the same Express app                                                        |
| Function duration            | `maxDuration` 60s, against a 55s internal budget for the model call, so the app answers before the platform cuts it off                                              |
| Security headers             | Nine headers declared once in `server/securityHeaders.ts`, sent by the API and repeated for the static host in `vercel.json`, with a test asserting both sides match |
| Rate limiting behind a proxy | Vercel sets its own marker, so the limiter trusts exactly one hop of `x-forwarded-for` and ignores anything the client wrote in front of it                          |
| Asset caching                | `/assets/*` is content-hashed and served `immutable` for a year; everything else is uncached                                                                         |
| Secrets                      | The key is read on the server only and never reaches the browser bundle; a scanner runs in CI                                                                        |

### Worth knowing

- **Region.** The function runs in Vercel's default region. If most of your traffic is in India, set
  the project's function region to `bom1` in _Settings → Functions_ — the model call is the slow
  part and it is made from there.
- **Rotate the key** if it has ever been pasted anywhere outside `.env`, before the site is public.
- **Free tier.** Everything here fits a Hobby project. The 60-second function ceiling is the only
  limit the application comes near.

## Environment variables

| Variable                | Required | Default               | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------- | -------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GEMINI_API_KEY`        | No\*     | —                     | A key from [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey). Without it the three assistant features answer "The planning assistant is not configured or not reachable right now." and the rest of the app is unaffected. |
| `GEMINI_MODEL`          | No       | `gemini-3.6-flash`    | The model asked first                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `GEMINI_FALLBACK_MODEL` | No       | `gemini-3.5-flash`    | The model tried if the first is unavailable or overloaded                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `PORT`                  | No       | `8787`                | The port the local API listens on. Ignored on Vercel.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS`    | No       | `0`, or `1` on Vercel | How many proxies sit in front. Every hop in `x-forwarded-for` beyond this count is written by the client and is ignored. Set it only if you know what is in front of you.                                                       |

\* Required only for the assistant features. A variable that is present but empty is treated as
absent.

## Scripts

| Script                  | What it does                                                                               |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `npm run dev`           | The web app on port 5180, with `/api` proxied to the local API                             |
| `npm run dev:api`       | The API on port 8787, reading `.env` if it exists                                          |
| `npm run build`         | The production build into `dist`                                                           |
| `npm run preview`       | Serves the built `dist` locally                                                            |
| `npm run verify`        | Types, lint, formatting, secret scan, tests with coverage thresholds, build, bundle budget |
| `npm run test`          | Unit and integration tests                                                                 |
| `npm run test:coverage` | The same, with coverage measured against the enforced floors                               |
| `npm run e2e`           | Browser tests on phone, tablet and desktop viewports                                       |
| `npm run lint`          | ESLint over everything                                                                     |
| `npm run format`        | Prettier, writing                                                                          |
| `npm run check:secrets` | Scans the working tree for anything that looks like a key                                  |
| `npm run check:size`    | Fails the build if the entry bundle crosses its gzipped budget                             |

`npm run verify` is the gate. If it passes, the branch is releasable.

## Architecture

```
shared/     the engine and the contracts, used by both sides
  engine/   expressions, conditions, model, diff, scheduler — pure functions
  packs/    wedding, birthday and community domain models, as data
  api.ts    one Zod schema per exchange: request, response, and the model's response format
  plan.ts   the stored shape, validated on every read
  redact.ts personal details stripped before anything is sent
src/        React 19 front end
  app/      the shell, routing and the plan page
  state/    a reducer, local persistence, and the twice-evaluated plan model
  features/ budget, timeline, vendors, ideas
  pages/    landing, about, privacy, new plan
  ui/       the small set of controls everything is built from
  styles/   design tokens, defined once per theme
server/     Express 5 API
  app.ts    the application: headers, limits, routes
  assistant.ts  the model calls, with a retry and fallback chain
  securityHeaders.ts  the nine headers, declared once
api/        one serverless entry point mounting the same app
e2e/        Playwright: journeys, accessibility, consistency, spacing, the checklist
scripts/    the secret scanner and the bundle-size gate
```

**Stack.** React 19, TypeScript in strict mode with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, Vite 8, Express 5,
Zod 4, Vitest and Playwright. No UI framework, no CSS framework, no state library — CSS Modules over
design tokens, and a reducer.

## Security

The API key is read on the server only and never reaches the browser bundle. Helmet with a strict
content security policy, a 32 kB body limit, `Cache-Control: no-store`, `strictObject` schemas that
reject unknown fields, and stable error codes that never carry a stack trace or an upstream message.
There are no accounts, no passwords and no user database, which removes that attack surface rather
than defending it.

Rate limiting is applied before body parsing and keyed on an address the client cannot write. A
proxy appends the address it saw to the end of `x-forwarded-for`, so only the last hops mean
anything; `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS` says how many proxies are actually in front, and defaults to none.
Reading the first hop instead — which is what Express's `trust proxy` does — let anyone rotate a
header and hold an unlimited number of budgets. The same nine headers are sent by the API and by the
static host, declared once in `server/securityHeaders.ts` and asserted on both sides by a test,
because a header set in one file and forgotten in the other is the usual way a policy rots.

To report a vulnerability, see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA is asserted by axe inside the browser tests, on every page, in light and dark mode, on
three viewports. There is a keyboard-only journey test and a reflow test at 320 px with text at
200%. Colour is never the only carrier of meaning, including in the spending ring. Motion is
decorative and is removed entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

What is **not** covered: no screen-reader pass has been recorded. axe cannot tell you whether a
screen makes sense read aloud, and that needs a person listening.

## Efficiency

The plan screens are code-split, so the landing page does not carry the engine. CI enforces a
gzipped budget measured from the Vite manifest, counting the entry chunk and its static imports
only. Both evaluations of a plan are memoised on the plan object, so moving a slider re-computes
once.

Measured on the production build: 6 requests, DOMContentLoaded in 103 ms, and a full recompute and
re-render on one lever move in 3.66 ms.

| `e2e/journey`        | Creating a plan, moving levers, recording a quote, reload, routing                            |
| `e2e/accessibility`  | axe at WCAG 2.2 AA on every page, both themes, three viewports                                |
| `e2e/consistency`    | One scroller in the application, one button scale, a dock that never moves what it reports on |

Coverage floors are enforced in CI and sit just under the real figures, so a change that drops
coverage fails the build rather than being noticed three commits later.

## Assumptions

- Cost rules are hand-authored planning estimates in rupees for an Indian city. They are defensible
  starting points, not price guarantees, and are meant to be replaced by real quotes.
- Not every guest attends every function of a wedding, so servings are modelled as a fraction of
  guests times functions rather than the whole product.
- For a community event, about 80% of expected attendance actually contributes when there is an
  entry fee, and one marshal per hundred attendees is the working safety rule.
- Plans are personal to one device. Nobody needs an account, so nobody gets one.

## Limitations

- Figures are estimates for one cost base. A different city or country will need different numbers,
  and the app does not know yours until you record a quote.
- Plans live in this browser's local storage. Clearing site data removes them, and they do not
  follow you to another device. There is no export yet.
- The interface is English only. The engine is language-neutral and the typography already carries
  Devanagari and Telugu, but the words are not translated yet.
- Ideas are generated by a language model and its costs are guesses. They are labelled as such.
- The packs cover the common shape of each event type. Anything unusual has to be recorded as a
  quote rather than modelled.
